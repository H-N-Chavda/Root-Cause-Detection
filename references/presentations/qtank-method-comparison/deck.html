Root cause detection

Finding cause and effect from plant data

Three methods tested on a four-tank rig where the right answer is already known.

PC · PCMCI+ · VAR-LiNGAM

28 September 2026

What we found

Three methods were given the same measurements and asked to work out what causes what.

Adopt

PCMCI+

  • Fewest mistakes on both settings
  • Never points an arrow backwards
  • Removes a signal’s own past before testing

Keep as a check

VAR-LiNGAM

  • Matches PCMCI+ when the signal is strong
  • Adds false links when it weakens
  • Works on a completely different principle

Retire

PC

  • Misses the same three links every time
  • A tank’s own past hides its real cause
  • Still useful as a baseline

All three already agree on how the pumps affect the tanks. They only disagree about how water moves between tanks.

Eight true links · two valve settings

The quadruple-tank system

Two pumps fill four tanks. A valve on each pump decides how the water splits.

Two pumps

  • We set the voltages v1 and v2
  • Each pump splits its water two ways
  • Part goes down its own side
  • The rest crosses to the opposite top tank

Four tanks

  • We measure all four levels, h1 to h4
  • Top tanks drain into the bottom ones
  • Water never flows back up

One disturbance

  • An extra inflow d into both top tanks
  • Stands in for an unplanned plant upset

The answer key

Easy to spot

  • Six arrows run from a pump or the disturbance straight into a tank
  • Strong, fast effects — every method finds all six

Hard to spot

  • Two arrows are top tanks draining into bottom tanks
  • Slow and weak — this is where the methods disagree

4

A tank draining itself is left out — true for all four, so it tells us nothing

What goes in, what comes out

All three methods take the same input and return the same kind of output.

Input

One table of numbers

  • A reading every 15 seconds for four hours
  • No equations, no diagram, no hints
  • We tell it to search 20 steps back

Output

A list of arrows

  • “This drives that, this many steps later”
  • Some arrows come back without a direction
  • The same link often repeats at several delays

Reading a causal graph

Four things to recognise before looking at any result.

Node

  • One signal we measure
  • Seven of them here

Edge

  • An arrow: one signal drives another
  • Eight of them in the answer key

Lag

  • How long the effect takes to show
  • We look up to 20 steps back

Self-loop

  • A tank affecting its own future level
  • True for every tank, so we leave it out

The three methods

Two questions separate them. The answers decide what each one can and cannot see.

PC

  • Checks if two signals stay linked
  • Ignores a signal’s own past

PCMCI+

  • Same check, but run twice
  • Strips out the past first

VAR-LiNGAM

  • Predicts, then studies the leftovers
  • Needs data that is not bell-shaped

Every arrow a method claims falls into one of four boxes.

Accuracy

Total mistakes

Where accuracy and total mistakes disagree, we trust total mistakes

Two valve settings, one much harder

Same rig, same eight links. Only the valve position changes.

P-minus

harder

  • Most water goes straight to the bottom tanks
  • Top tanks sit nearly empty and barely move
  • So the drains they feed leave almost no trace

P-plus

easier

  • Most water passes through the top tanks
  • They fill higher and swing about twice as wide
  • So the drains are easy to see

Textbook theory says P-plus should be the harder one. It is not. What matters is simply how much water reaches the tanks we need to watch.

Why PC misses the same links

Its failures are not random — the same three links go missing every time.

What goes wrong

  • A tank fills slowly, so its level barely changes
  • Each reading almost repeats the one before
  • That drowns out the weaker real cause

What PCMCI+ does instead

  • It removes the tank’s own past first
  • Then looks for what is left
  • That one step is the whole difference

PC misses the identical three links on both settings. A blind spot that ignores the data is a fault in the method.

What we recommend

The direction is clear. The limits are worth stating plainly.

Adopt

PCMCI+

  • Fewest mistakes on both settings
  • Can be told what we already know

Keep as a check

VAR-LiNGAM

  • Works on a different principle
  • Not safe to use on its own

Retire

PC

  • Same blind spot on both settings
  • Keep it only as a baseline

What we cannot yet say

  • Only eight links — one changes the score noticeably
  • This rig has no control loop; real plants do
  • All three methods assumed straight-line effects

Suggested next step

  • Repeat on a rig that includes a control loop
  • That is the condition every real plant has
  • And the one gap this work does not cover
