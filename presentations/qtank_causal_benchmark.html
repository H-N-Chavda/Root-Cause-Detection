The datasets

Two datasets because the same rig behaves two different ways depending on how the valves split the flow

Same 8 true arrows and same inputs in both: 4 h at 5 s sampling, 2880 rows each. Only the strength and timing of each arrow changes.

P− · minimum phase · the "easy" control case

Lower-tank level after a pump step: rises promptly

  • Most of each pump's flow goes straight to the lower tanks
  • Drainage links carry the smaller share of water
  • Time constants 23–91 s

P+ · non-minimum phase · the "hard" control case

Lower-tank level after a pump step: briefly moves the wrong way, then follows

  • Most of each pump's flow goes to the upper tanks first
  • Drainage links carry the larger share of water
  • Time constants 39–91 s

4

How we score

Every algorithm hands back a graph; we count how many of the 8 true arrows it found, missed, flipped or invented

The 7 signals

v

v1, v2: pump voltages. Driven by us, square waves

d

d: disturbance flow into the upper tanks

h

h1–h4: tank levels. The outputs the algorithms must explain

All three inputs are recorded, so nothing hidden drives two tanks at once.

Three scores

Precision: of the arrows reported, how many are real

Recall: of the 8 real arrows, how many were reported, pointing the right way

F1: the balance of the two. 1.0 means a perfect graph

SHD: number of edits (add, delete, flip) to reach the true graph. Lower is better

Lagged results are collapsed to one arrow per pair before scoring. Self-loops are ignored.

Shared settings

Significance: 0.01

Time window: 20 steps (100 s), about one time constant of the slowest tank

Test: partial correlation (linear)

VAR-LiNGAM: 100 bootstrap resamples, keep a link only if present in 90 of them

Row count used by the main run is not recorded in its summary. UNVERIFIED whether it used all 2880 rows.

Source: datasets/score_runs.py; results/qtank/run-parcorr-tau20/run_summary.json

5

The methods

Three ways of asking "does A drive B?", each with a different blind spot

PC

Removes a link whenever two signals become unrelated once you control for the others.

Built for snapshots, not time series. We ran it twice: with no time window, and with the same 20-step window as the others.

Blind spot: no correction for signals that mostly predict themselves

PCMCI+

PC redesigned for time series. Tests each link at every lag in the window and corrects for autocorrelation.

The method the literature recommends for slow, autocorrelated process data. Our pre-registered favourite.

Blind spot: linear test only, in this run

VAR-LiNGAM

Fits a linear forecasting model, then uses the shape of the noise (non-Gaussian) to decide which way each link points.

Keeps a link only if it survives 90 of 100 bootstrap resamples.

Blind spot: direction is unreliable where the noise is Gaussian (the tank levels)

Source: src/causal_bench/algorithms/pc.py, pcmci_plus.py, var_lingam.py; docs/QTP_EXPECTATIONS.md

VAR-LiNGAM matches PCMCI+ at P+ but pays about 90× the runtime and stumbles on direction at P−

At P+: all 8 true links, 2 invented (F1 0.89)

Ties PCMCI+ on F1 and SHD. The two extras are cross-pump links (v1→h2, v2→h1).

At P−: one link reversed, one missed (F1 0.67)

Reports h1→h3 instead of h3→h1 and drops h4→h2. Both are drainage links.

Why direction fails on the tanks

Its direction rule needs non-Gaussian noise. The pump signals have it; the tank levels are near-Gaussian because the tanks smooth everything. Predicted before the run.

Where the time goes

100 bootstrap fits with 20 lags each. Not inherent to the estimator; a cheaper setting would cut it.

12

Implications

What this means for root-cause detection on real process data

1

Use PCMCI+ as the default

Best accuracy at both operating points, seconds to run, and it never reversed a link in any run.

2

Distrust links between two signals on the same actuator

Shared-pump tanks produced a spurious link in 5 of 6 runs. Check such links against the plant drawing before acting.

3

Expect slow, weak couplings to go missing

Tank-to-tank drainage was the hard case every time. Where such links are known, feed them in as prior knowledge.

Confidence limits: one simulator, two operating points, one random seed, linear tests only. No error bars exist on any number in this deck.
